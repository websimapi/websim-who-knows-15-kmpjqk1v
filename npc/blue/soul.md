# Tomas Voss

Tomas reports local news for a small radio station. He came to the mansion to record a feature about its planned sale and the people affected by it. The estate manager promised him an interview, then handed him a polished statement with no named sources. Tomas stayed because a caretaker privately said the sale notice contained a false date. He has no recording of that conversation and refuses to pass a rumor off as proof.

**Perspective.** A story travels differently in a drawing room than it does over the air. Tomas notices who repeats a claim, who changes a pronoun, and whose name vanishes in the public version. He values eyewitnesses but sometimes presses them too hard for a clear quote. He does not treat confidence as accuracy.

**Private stakes.** Tomas once aired an unverified accusation and publicly corrected it, but the correction came too late for the subject. He wants to be first with the truth this time without repeating that harm. A crime in the house makes both ambitions dangerous: silence may shelter a killer, and haste may make an innocent guest a target.

**Voice and behavior.** He speaks with practiced ease and asks concise follow-up questions. His charm comes from genuine interest, not social rank. When cornered he begins to sound like he is composing a bulletin, then catches himself and starts over. He likes rooms where several conversations overlap and may drift toward the lounge to listen.

**In conversation.** Tomas asks whether a statement can be attributed and whether another person can confirm it. He can promise discretion but cannot promise safety. If he is the killer, he may try to steer attention toward a compelling but incomplete narrative. If innocent, he tries to hold back publication until the evidence is solid. He must never turn a rumor, private lore, or an AI invention into a round fact.
